# Making sense of it

Four pages read the same data in four different ways. All of them are driven by
one time range and sport filter at the top.

## Dashboard

Stat cards with period-on-period change and sparklines, your training goals,
personal bests, this week against your usual week, training load, and the piece
of gear closest to needing replacement.

### Goals and streaks

Add as many goals as you like under **Settings → Training goals**. A goal counts
activities ("two runs of at least 5 km a week"), distance ("40 km of hiking a
month") or time ("30 hours of running a month"), over a window of one or more
weeks or months.

Each is tracked on its own: a streak of windows that met the target, a progress
bar for the window in hand, and a trophy once it is done. The window in progress
can extend a streak but never breaks one — a quiet Monday costs you nothing.

Four card styles are available (Standard, Rings, Ledger, and Today's move, which
names the one thing that would keep you on track). They show the same numbers.

## Analysis

Four tabs under one filter:

- **Records** — personal bests by sport and distance.
- **Trends** — how distance, pace, heart rate and volume are moving.
- **Efficiency** — efficiency factor: output against the heart rate it cost.
- **Load** — acute against chronic load. Around 1.0 means this week matches
  what your body is used to; higher is building, lower is easing off. It only
  appears with six weeks of history and a dozen heart-rate activities, because
  below that one session swings it wildly. Read it as a description of your
  load, not a medical verdict.

## Consistency

Whether you are showing up, rather than how fast:

- **Activity calendar** — one cell per day, shaded by count or time. Unbroken
  runs of colour are streaks; the blanks are where it slipped.
- **Day of week** — which days you actually train, as opposed to intend to.
- **Training sessions** — gym work by week, split into sessions.
- **Year over year**, **week over week**, and **cumulative distance**.

## Equipment

Shoes, bikes, watches and the rest, with the workouts logged against each and
the distance they have covered. Give a piece of gear a replacement distance
(shoes default to 600 km) and the dashboard nudges you once it passes 80% of it.
Gear that does not wear by distance is never nudged.

## Reading the charts

Every chart has an **info** icon next to its title with a longer explanation of
what it measures, how to read it, and the caveats worth knowing before acting on
it. Hover on desktop, tap on mobile.
